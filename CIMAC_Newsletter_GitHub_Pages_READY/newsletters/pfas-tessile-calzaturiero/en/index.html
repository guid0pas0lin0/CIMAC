PFAS in textiles and footwear: why businesses should act now

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) have long been used in textiles and footwear to impart water repellency, oil repellency and stain resistance. Their high environmental persistence has prompted closer regulatory scrutiny and growing pressure to restrict their use.

Where might PFAS be found?

Potential applications include technical textiles, waterproof and breathable membranes, treated leather, synthetic materials, protective coatings, yarns and components for outdoor, sports and professional products. PFAS may also be introduced through finishing treatments, making their presence difficult to establish without reliable supply-chain information or testing.

Why prepare for regulatory change?

Brands and distributors increasingly require information on chemical composition and substances of concern. Supply-chain transparency helps companies prepare for regulatory changes and customer requirements.

Practical steps for businesses

Identify potentially affected materials and components; obtain updated supplier declarations; assess PFAS using appropriate testing and technical documentation; explore alternatives; and monitor European regulatory developments.

How CIMAC can help

Targeted laboratory testing and a review of supplier documentation can help identify PFAS-related risks in materials and components.
