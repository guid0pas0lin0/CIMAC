PFAS in textiles and footwear: why preparation matters now

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) have long been used in textiles and footwear to provide water resistance, repellency and stain protection. Their environmental persistence has led to greater scrutiny and evolving regulatory expectations.

Where PFAS may occur

Potential applications include technical textiles, waterproof and breathable membranes, treated leather, synthetic materials, protective coatings, yarns and components for outdoor, sports and professional products. Some PFAS are introduced during manufacturing treatments and may not be immediately apparent in finished products.

Why act before further restrictions

Brands and distributors increasingly request information on product chemical composition and the presence of substances of concern. Supply-chain transparency helps companies prepare for regulatory changes and customer requirements.

Practical steps for businesses

Identify potentially affected materials and components; obtain updated supplier declarations; assess PFAS using appropriate testing and technical documentation; explore alternatives; and monitor European regulatory developments.

How CIMAC can help

Laboratory testing and document reviews can support material screening and help companies evaluate exposure to PFAS-related compliance risks.
